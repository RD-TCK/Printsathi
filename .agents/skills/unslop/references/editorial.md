# Editorial guidance

UNSLOP skill 0.9.0. Use these independently authored heuristics while drafting, editing or reviewing English prose, even before account connection. Local edits use the current agent and are not Jev-verified. A requested checked rewrite follows SKILL.md and sends the original to the server. These heuristics are not extra API rubric categories, a forbidden-word list or evidence of AI authorship.

## Before delivering prose

1. Identify the reader, purpose and facts already supplied. A personal note needs no product pitch; technical prose may need precise terminology.
2. Cut opening or closing framing that delays or repeats the actual point. Keep setup that gives necessary context.
3. Combine repeated propositions while retaining each fact, exception and qualification at least once.
4. Replace vague phrasing only with details already supplied. Ask for a missing fact; never invent a number, example or claim.
5. Read the passage for rhythm and clear actors. Prefer direct wording when it helps; preserve useful passive voice, punctuation, questions, adverbs and deliberate repetition.
6. Compare with the original for names, quantities, dates, uncertainty, humor and voice. A shorter sentence is not automatically a better sentence.

Keep this a short editorial pass, not a numeric self-score or an endless rewrite loop. Keep clean writing unchanged. Apply feedback locally when requested, but do not label it as checked by UNSLOP's API. If a checked UNSLOP revision is requested, retain and submit the user's original baseline rather than silently sending a locally polished substitute. Ordinary drafting never triggers a background paid request.

## Judge the passage's job

| Notice | Consider an edit when | Preserve when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interchangeable opening | The hook could introduce unrelated products and delays the actual observation. | An opening establishes a real question, scene or audience constraint. |
| Repeated conclusion | Later wording adds no distinct fact, qualification or useful emphasis. | Repetition creates purposeful rhythm, instruction or humor. |
| Grand promotional language | Certainty or scale exceeds support supplied by the author. | A supported claim is strong, or enthusiasm is intentionally personal. Missing support does not prove a claim false. |
| Mechanical contrast | A contrast implies a distinction the passage does not explain. | The alternatives are materially different and the contrast helps the reader choose. |
| Predictable lists or transitions | The structure expands a simple point without helping the reader act. | A real sequence, comparison or parallel set is easier to follow this way. |
| Generic ending | It repeats the promise or adds a call to action unrelated to the author's purpose. | A specific next step tells the reader what to do and why. |

Inspect familiar phrases in context; familiarity alone is not a defect. Do not mechanically ban punctuation, sentence lengths, contractions, rhetorical questions or words. Specificity can come from a concrete task, constraint or audience; do not demand numbers where none are needed. Preserve jargon that the audience needs, quotations, jokes and the user's intentional voice.

## Original examples

These are authored illustrations, not observed social posts, model outputs, factual claims about UNSLOP or test results. The revision uses only information already present in each draft.

**Remove framing; keep the qualification.**

Draft: “A whole new chapter in planning starts here. For teams of up to four, the planner may reduce Friday scheduling work by placing shift requests in one view.”

Revision: “For teams of up to four, the planner may reduce Friday scheduling work by placing shift requests in one view.”

The team-size condition and “may” survive. Do not replace them with a universal time-saving claim.

**Combine the duplicate; retain the limitation.**

Draft: “The export includes up to 200 rows. You can export as many as 200 rows. Images are excluded.”

Revision: “The export includes up to 200 rows. Images are excluded.”

The row cap and exclusion matter more than making the copy shorter.

**Keep a useful contrast.**

Draft: “The alert reaches the on-call engineer, not the whole company.”

Keep it. The contrast defines who receives a notification; deleting it would lose a constraint.

**Keep deliberate rhythm.**

Draft: “I tried it Monday. Broke it Tuesday. Fixed it Wednesday.”

Keep it when this is the author's chosen story and voice. Parallel structure is doing useful work.

**Ask for evidence instead of inventing specificity.**

Draft: “Every customer doubles revenue in a week.”

If no support is supplied, ask what results and population the author can substantiate. Do not invent a percentage, testimonial or softer substitute result. A concern about support is not external fact-checking.

## Use alongside another writing skill

If the user already uses stop-slop or another editor, keep that tool's contribution distinct. Retain the user's original before any edits. Do not silently run a preliminary rewrite and submit only that edited version: UNSLOP could then verify preservation against the wrong baseline.

For a requested comparison, send the original to UNSLOP and compare the separate candidates against that same original. Identify which result passed UNSLOP's server checks; another tool's candidate is not UNSLOP-verified. A check of an already edited draft cannot establish fidelity to an unseen original. If only an edited draft is available, explain this limitation and obtain the original before making that claim. Do not launch extra paid calls merely to compare tools.

For short spoken marketing copy, notice breath, clear referents and words that are comfortable to say. These are contextual observations within marketing_copy, not dedicated TikTok/YouTube scoring, a virality forecast or permission to publish. Do not turn a personal X post into a product pitch unless requested.
